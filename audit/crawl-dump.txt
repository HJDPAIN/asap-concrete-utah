1. Contact local city offices, or building inspector to determine what codes are in place for egress windows. Certain codes require a minimum size the window needs to be and the windows distance from the floor. Contractors may also be able to help you figure out egress window codes.

 2. Hire a contractor.  Adding an egress window may seem like it would be an easy project, but you may be surprised how many steps it takes and how much expertise is involved. Some sizes of windows may also require an engineer to inspect and approve the changes you're making in the homes foundation. Hiring a trusted contractor to manage the project will make the process much easier for you. 

3. Decide what window size you want and where exactly you want the window. Dig out the dirt where the window well will be.  Have the contractor or window installer mark out exactly where the window needs to be. 

 4. Hire a concrete cutter to come cut in the opening if you are installing a new window or making an existing window bigger. Give them as much info about the job as possible.  Such as job location, concrete thickness, window size, etc.  We are also able to help remove the concrete upon request. 

5. Install window, place window well, fill window well with gravel, back fill around window well, and do any finish work, waterproofing, or trim that you need to finish up the project.

Does Your Concrete Need To Be Sealed?
January 10, 2023|Concrete Sealer

      Sealing concrete is an important step to keep your driveways, sidewalks, and concrete pads in great shape.  If neglected, your concrete can quickly lose its finished surface.  When the surface of your concrete starts to chip up it is called "spalling". This can be caused by a few things. First, is road salts. The salts used to remove ice and snow have a damaging effect on your concrete. The chemicals in the salts get into the surface of your concrete and loosen the bonds of the concrete and weaken it. It is recommended to not put salts on your driveway for at least the first 3 years after it is poured.

       Second, is freeze/thaw cycles. Winters in Utah can be brutal and the temperatures can be unpredictable. Concrete is porous so water can seep into the surface of the concrete. When water gets into the surface of your concrete and freezes, the water expands and puts pressure on the concrete causing it to chip up. The combination of freeze/thaw cycles and road salts can be a nightmare for your concrete. 

       Example of what spalling or chipped concrete looks like. 




So how do you prevent your concrete from spalling? 

        The answer is simple. Avoid road salts, and apply a concrete sealer! There are many types of sealers that can be used based on how old the concrete is, or the type of finish that was done on it. For most outdoor concrete, a penetrating concrete sealer is best. This is what we mainly use on our customer's driveways. A penetrating sealer seeps into the surface of your concrete and creates a protective barrier to repel water, oil, road salts, and more. This sealer makes your driveway easier to clean if you happen to spill oil or other substances onto it. 

     The sealer we use does not change the color, texture, or sheen of your concrete.  Since this sealer repels water is prevents the freeze/thaw cycles by keeping the water on the surface of the concrete instead of it seeping in. We use a power washer to clean your driveway before we apply the sealer to ensure that it works properly. 

  Keeping your concrete looking great can be so simple but it is often neglected. A great looking driveway always adds curb appeal to your home or business. 

 Be sure to reach out to us if you'd like to have your concrete sealed. We are happy to help you get it done!

Core Drilling Tips and Tricks
February 7, 2021|Core Drilling

Core drilling can be easy if you have the right equipment, AND a good core drill bit. In the world of concrete cutting and core drilling, the more you spend on a bit or blade, the better it will perform.  There are plenty of different kinds of core bits to choose from. You can get bits that drill fast, but don't last as long (Usually more expensive). Bits that drill slow, but last a long time. And bits in the middle that will drill fairly quick and have longer life.  My favorite are the ones that drill fast. The faster you drill, the faster you get done and can move onto the next job. 

I've learned a few tricks on how to make core bits drill faster.  If you are core drilling and the bit doesn't seem to be drilling or is taking forever to drill, I've removed a segment or two off of the bit. This will decrease the surface area and allow the diamonds in the segment to put more pressure on the concrete. To remove a segment all you need is a hammer, pair of pliers, or just pinch an adjustable wrench on the segment and move it back and forth until the weld breaks. 

Another option if you are struggling to drill in concrete is to try drilling block, brick, or asphalt for a little bit. These more abrasive materials will open up the diamonds in your segments and allow for quicker drilling in concrete. This also works with blades. After I cut asphalt, my blades cut much quicker in concrete. 

Most core bits require water on them while drilling. This helps keep the bit cool, and pushes the sediment out of where your bit is drilling so the bit can keep it's speed. If you drill without water it could quickly ruin your bit. 

One more thing you can try if the bit is struggling to drill is to add a little bit of sand into the hole where your bit is drilling. This will be more abrasive and hopefully open up the segments so the diamonds can continue to cut.

Locating In-Floor Heating
January 7, 2021|Concrete Scanning

In-Floor Heating Before Concrete Is Poured In Basement

In-Floor Heating can be a great addition when building a new home. These tubes have heated water running through them to keep your floors nice and warm.

A problem you don't want is to have the lines penetrated. If one of these lines get penetrated, you are in for a very expensive and difficult repair.  

If you need to drill or anchor into concrete with in-floor heating underneath, it's best to know where these lines are and to stay as far away as possible from them. 

Give us a call before you drill so we can help you locate these lines with our concrete scanner. The scanner will also show the thickness of the concrete so you know how deep you can drill before getting to the lines.

Concrete Scanning
July 12, 2020|Concrete Scanning

       We recently added concrete scanning to our services. This piece of equipment allows us to take a look inside and under the concrete. This scanner can detect conduit, pipe, post-tension cables, electrical wire, concrete thickness, wire mesh, and rebar!  It is important to know what is under the concrete to make your project go smoothly. Scanning concrete can save you money and help you plan your project. Cutting through unexpected conduit can add an extra expense that you might not have had in the bid for your project. It is also important to know what structural reinforcement you have in your concrete for remodels to ensure that the structure is stable.
