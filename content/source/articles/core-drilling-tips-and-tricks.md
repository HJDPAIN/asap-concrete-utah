Core Drilling Tips and Tricks
February 7, 2021|Core Drilling

Core drilling can be easy if you have the right equipment, AND a good core drill bit. In the world of concrete cutting and core drilling, the more you spend on a bit or blade, the better it will perform.  There are plenty of different kinds of core bits to choose from. You can get bits that drill fast, but don't last as long (Usually more expensive). Bits that drill slow, but last a long time. And bits in the middle that will drill fairly quick and have longer life.  My favorite are the ones that drill fast. The faster you drill, the faster you get done and can move onto the next job. 

I've learned a few tricks on how to make core bits drill faster.  If you are core drilling and the bit doesn't seem to be drilling or is taking forever to drill, I've removed a segment or two off of the bit. This will decrease the surface area and allow the diamonds in the segment to put more pressure on the concrete. To remove a segment all you need is a hammer, pair of pliers, or just pinch an adjustable wrench on the segment and move it back and forth until the weld breaks. 

Another option if you are struggling to drill in concrete is to try drilling block, brick, or asphalt for a little bit. These more abrasive materials will open up the diamonds in your segments and allow for quicker drilling in concrete. This also works with blades. After I cut asphalt, my blades cut much quicker in concrete. 

Most core bits require water on them while drilling. This helps keep the bit cool, and pushes the sediment out of where your bit is drilling so the bit can keep it's speed. If you drill without water it could quickly ruin your bit. 

One more thing you can try if the bit is struggling to drill is to add a little bit of sand into the hole where your bit is drilling. This will be more abrasive and hopefully open up the segments so the diamonds can continue to cut.
